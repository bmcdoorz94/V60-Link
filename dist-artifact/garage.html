Data lab

Load a log from a scanner app and the lab checks warm-up, fuel trims, charging, idle and boost, then charts every channel on a shared time axis. This page cannot talk to the car's port itself, so the car-to-page link is an exported file.

How to get a log from the car
  1. Plug a Bluetooth or Wi-Fi ELM327-type OBD-II adapter into the port under the dash.
  2. Use a scanner app that records data to a file, such as Car Scanner, Torque or OBD Fusion, and log at least coolant temperature, RPM, speed, fuel trims, battery voltage and intake pressure.
  3. Drive a normal mix: cold start, some city, some steady highway, and one firm acceleration if safe.
  4. Export the log as CSV and load it below. Both one-column-per-channel files and files with PID and VALUE columns are read. If a channel is missed, the mapping step lets you pick the column.

Logging is read-only. Nothing here writes to the car or tunes the engine.

              2. Clear codes without wrecking your inspection

              • Texas dropped safety inspections for personal cars on January 1, 2025, but Travis County still requires an emissions test.
              • The test reads the readiness monitors. A 2001 or newer car may have at most one monitor not ready. Any check-engine light with a stored code fails.
              • Clearing codes, or disconnecting the battery, resets every monitor. A generic way to complete them: a cold start, stop-and-go driving up to about 30 mph, then 5 to 7 minutes at a steady 55 mph, repeated over a few days. Some evaporative-system tests need the car to sit about 8 hours first. A Volvo-specific drive cycle could not be confirmed.
              • Before an inspection, run a health check in V60 Link or Car Scanner and confirm readiness first.
              Clearing is for confirming a repair, not for hiding a fault. Texas makes manipulating an emissions test result an offense.

              How it works

              The T5 is a small four-cylinder that uses a turbocharger to make the power of a bigger engine. The path air and oil take explains most of what goes wrong and why the maintenance list looks the way it does.

              Air in

              Air passes the filter, gets squeezed by the compressor, and is cooled in the intercooler so it packs more oxygen. The throttle meters it into the cylinders.

              Exhaust out

              Exhaust spins the turbine, which drives the compressor on a shared shaft. The turbine runs very hot and is lubricated by engine oil, so oil level and quality matter more on a turbo engine.

              Crankcase ventilation

              Some combustion gas always slips past the piston rings. The PCV system pulls those vapors back into the intake to be burned. If it clogs, crankcase pressure rises and oil can be pushed out of seals or drawn into the intake.

              Oil

              Oil lubricates, cools the pistons and feeds the turbo. Spec for your car, per Volvo's 2016 US manual: full synthetic ACEA A5/B5 SAE 5W-30, or Volvo's 0W-20 (VCC RBS0-2AE), such as Castrol Edge Professional. 6.2 US quarts (5.9 L) with the filter.

              Cooling

              The thermostat decides when coolant starts flowing through the radiator. Brake fluid absorbs moisture over time, which is why it is replaced on a calendar and not by miles.

              Automatic transmission

              An Aisin 8-speed. Volvo's published documents list no scheduled fluid change for normal driving, and some independent shops service it anyway. Use the exact fluid your gearbox variant needs and ask a dealer to confirm it by VIN.

              Known trouble

              Oil consumption is the big one. Volvo issued service bulletins and extended-warranty programs for excessive oil consumption on 2015 and 2016 Drive-E engines, with a repair that replaces pistons and piston rings. The extended coverage was limited to 8 years from delivery or 100,000 miles, so on a 2016 it may already have run out. I could not confirm from the public bulletins whether your exact engine is on the covered list. Ask Volvo customer care or a dealer to check eligibility by VIN, mention your oil log, and ask about goodwill coverage.

              Clogged PCV

              One owner review describes oil burning at 110,000 miles traced to a clogged PCV system. That is a single account, not a failure rate, but it is a cheap thing to have inspected.

              Thermostat

              Owner comments on Consumer Reports and Edmunds mention thermostat failures. The same Edmunds reviewer says it is costly mainly because the intake has to come off. Anecdotal.

              Low oil warning

              A Volvo service bulletin covers a low oil level message on 2015 and 2016 Drive-E cars and points to piston and ring replacement when consumption stays high. If it appears, check the oil level in the instrument cluster right away and log it.

              Glossary

              Drive-E
              Volvo's family name for its newer 4-cylinder engines. Yours is the 2.0 L turbo petrol.
              Turbocharger
              A pump driven by exhaust gas that forces extra air into the engine.
              Intercooler
              A small radiator for the compressed air, since squeezing air heats it.
              PCV
              Positive crankcase ventilation. Routes crankcase vapors back into the intake.
              Piston rings
              Seals around each piston that keep combustion gas in and oil out of the cylinder.
              Fuel trim
              How much the engine computer adds or removes fuel to hit the right mixture. Short-term (STFT) reacts fast, long-term (LTFT) is the learned correction.
              MAF
              Mass air flow sensor. Measures air entering the engine.
              MAP
              Manifold absolute pressure. Intake pressure, which rises above outside air pressure under boost.
              Thermostat
              A valve that holds coolant back until the engine is warm.
              ATF
              Automatic transmission fluid.
              OBD-II
              The standard diagnostic port under the dash.
              VIDA
              Volvo's dealer software for service intervals, bulletins and procedures. Volvo's own sheets treat it as the primary source for intervals.
              TSB
              Technical service bulletin. A manufacturer notice to dealers about a known problem and its fix. Not the same as a recall.
              Extended warranty program
              Coverage a manufacturer adds for a specific known defect after the normal warranty ends, usually with its own time and mileage limits.
